Days before the midterm, the Democratic Party appears set to gain seats in the U.S. House of Representatives. But there’s considerable debate over the size of their lead, and questions about the accuracy of polls linger in the wake of Republicans outperforming polls in 2024. At Ipsos we’re committed to making polling as accurate as possible and fixing the bias that has surfaced in polls at various points since 2016. This fall we launched a major R&D initiative to make polling more accurate by evaluating how different data collection approaches influence survey participation and political representation. Our largest investment was a national 100% mail survey sampled from the L2 voter file. Mail delivery began Oct. 9, and we received 1,318 completions from Oct. 20-28. While most poll response rates are around 1%, this one’s was significantly higher (9%). The poll estimates Democrats’ advantage in the generic U.S. House vote at 6 points (50% Dem candidate to 44% Rep candidate), based on the 1,209 likely voters interviewed (MoE +/- 3.5 pp). That margin is roughly 3 points less than polling aggregators are showing. The most important takeaway is not any single result. Rather, it is what studies like this can tell us about participation patterns, representativeness, and the ongoing challenge of reducing survey error. Why a mail survey? (1) Paper questionnaires have proven useful for reaching a more politically representative sample; (2) People can respond without joining a panel; (3) We can demonstrate the poll’s credibility and distinguish it from spam using a high-end envelope containing a cash incentive. This mail survey used a short questionnaire (5 minutes), a $1 or $5 pre-incentive (randomized) in a UPS Mail Innovations envelope, and a $10 post-paid incentive. Is the poll perfect? No, no poll is, and every approach has strengths and limitations. Mail surveys can achieve stronger engagement among some groups, while probability-based online panels such as Ipsos’ KnowledgePanel offer important advantages in speed, scale, efficiency, and the ability to rapidly measure public opinion over time. One of the most exciting aspects of this work is exploring how insights gained from studies like this can inform and further enhance KnowledgePanel and other Ipsos research solutions. By evaluating multiple methodologies side-by-side, we're continuing to invest in the science of survey research and helping clients make better-informed decisions with greater confidence. This winter we’ll be exploring ways to combine this type of poll with the KnowledgePanel to see if a hybrid approach can offer even greater insight. Stay tuned for more research coming out of this novel R&D effort. Big thanks to all my colleagues who supported and/or worked on this project.
