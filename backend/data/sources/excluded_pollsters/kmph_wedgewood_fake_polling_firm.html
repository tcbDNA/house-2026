Fake polling firm tricked media, claims fabricated surveys were for 'social experiment'

by NATALIA MITTELSTADT | The National News Desk

Updated Thu, October 22, 2026 at 8:03 AM

WASHINGTON (TNND) — A fake polling firm tricked several media outlets with fabricated surveys, claiming they were part of a "social experiment," angering Democrats.

Wedgewood Polling on Tuesday released a statement on X saying that it was an "experimental project to demonstrate the vulnerabilities, lack of oversight, and overall low barrier to entry in the modern political polling industry."

The group said it was "far too easy" for their polls to "gain traction" and "be taken seriously."

"Despite having no history, formal credentials, or real data behind the vast majority of our releases, Wedgewood Polling was added to major polling aggregators, including The New York Times, FiftyPlusOne, and VoteHub, and repeatedly treated as a legitimate data source," the statement reads.

"Our fabricated numbers were given instant credibility across national media platforms—including being cited on air and in reporting by outlets such as MS NOW. The ease with which unverified figures were absorbed into mainstream political narratives highlights a systemic breakdown in media vetting and data aggregation."

Wedgewood said that only one Nebraska poll, which was crowdfunded, was legitimate.

"Wedgewood Polling was never intended to sway an election or permanently mislead the public; it was designed as a wake-up call," the group added.

The disclosure comes as the head of the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) posted on X about a surge in, "entirely Fake Polls and Suppression Polls" meant to interfere with elections.

Michigan Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed's campaign cited the group's fake polls amid the primary race against Michigan state Sen. Mallory McMorrow and U.S. House Rep. Haley Stevens, Fox News reported. Political Polls, a nonpartisan polling aggregator, also cited Wedgewood in the Wisconsin gubernatorial race, showing Democratic Socialists of America member Francesca Hong leading Republican candidate Rep. Tom Tiffany.

McMorrow responded to Wedgewood's statement on Tuesday, saying that the group's June poll of her race led to donations drying up, "devastated" volunteers, and reporters who "hounded" her with calls to drop out of the race.

"Sure, a point was proven. Polling is broken. But tens of thousands of people poured their hearts, time, energy, hard-earned money into something they believed in - for a year-and-a-half, and it was treated like a game. Our volunteers gave their all. And then some. I felt like I had let so many of them down," McMorrow wrote.

"Trust is already at an all-time low. We don't get out of this mess without rebuilding trust. And media - who loves to ride the drama of a pollercoaster - needs to take a hard look at what's legitimate and what's not. If this teaches us anything - DON'T GET COMPLACENT AND DON'T TRUST THE POLLS," she added.

View post on X

Democrat strategist Jennifer Holdsworth told Fox News she ran a super PAC that fundraised for McMorrow and that "this poll was used by several donors to pull back funding at a time we were being outspent by $80 million."

Other Wedgewood polls showed Democrats with significant leads over Republicans in states like Texas, where a July survey showed Democratic Senate candidate James Talarico leading state Attorney General Ken Paxton among Hispanics by 64% to 30%.

The New York Times and Decision Desk HQ both cited Wedgewood, but have since said they removed them from their projections.

After Wedgewood admitted to the fake data, The Times said it removed 15 of the group's polls released between June and October. The newspaper said Wedgewood had "answered a series of questions regarding the identities of the pollsters; the methods they were using to draw their sample, contact respondents and weight their polls; and the source of their funding" during its vetting process.

Decision Desk HQ said 17 Wedgewood polls were removed from its projections but that it "has not changed the projected seat totals or chamber control probabilities in the Senate or House."

Democratic strategist and former Kamala Harris advisor Mike Nellis told Fox News regarding Wedgewood, "These people are absolute scumbags. They can try to claim that they have some moral high ground here, but they lied about their polling data, got caught, and are freaking out because they've committed fraud."

"The people responsible for this should be run out of American politics, and if they can be, they should be prosecuted to the full extent of the law," Nellis said.

He added that Democrats, Republicans, and the media should reflect on how polling is verified "because there's likely other people that are doing things like this."

A group called "Wedgewood Polls" had previously created a GoFundMe to fundraise for polling in Nebraska's gubernatorial and Senate races, raising $500 of its intended $5,000.

After Wedgewood released its statement on Tuesday, FiftyPlusOne co-founder G. Elliott Morris posted on X, "We were investigating Wedgewood for faking their data and emailed them for comment this morning, ahead of publishing the findings tomorrow. They are trying to cover up their fraud with the 'we were doing a social experiment' excuse."

Morris also shared a screenshot of a direct message he received from Wedgewood, which says, "Our official response to your article: F*** you," with the next words redacted. FiftyPlusOne said on Substack that the redacted words were "the name of an individual not affiliated with FiftyPlusOne."

View post on X

FiftyPlusOne added that Wedgewood's "statement alleges that FiftyPlusOne, the New York Times, and other poll aggregators did not conduct appropriate vetting of their firm prior to aggregating their polls; this is false. We reached out to them to ask who they were and confirm the methodology statement on their website."

"Wedgewood operated as if it were a legitimate polling firm, including collecting money from donors to conduct crowd-funded polls, and sharing raw survey data (which we now have confirmed was fraudulent) with FiftyPlusOne on Oct. 15, 2026," according to the Substack.

Wedgewood's admission comes after a polling firm closed in August, following its release of fake election surveys for the Los Angeles mayoral race and the Wisconsin Democratic gubernatorial primary. That polling firm, Median Strategies, admitted that it was releasing fake polls for a "social experiment."
