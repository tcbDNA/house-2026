How this is built
Starting point: the 2024 US Senate race (Allred v Cruz) for Senate and the 2022 Governor race (O’Rourke v Abbott) for Governor, county by county, from the Texas Secretary of State.
Votes so far: county voter rosters for Harris, Dallas, Bexar, Travis, Tarrant, Collin, Denton, Fort Bend, Hidalgo, Williamson and Montgomery; the Secretary of State’s daily county counts for the other 243. Days a county did not report are filled from its own recent pace. Mail ballots are estimated from 2024 where the state shows almost none. Titus, Hale, Hardeman and Young had past-year counts corrected against official results.
Expected total: votes so far times how much each county grew from this point to Election Day in 2024, in 2022, or halfway between (the default, which comes closest to the Harris County forecast).
Who’s showing up: each county’s expected share of the state’s two-candidate vote against its share in the starting race. The turnout effects add up exactly to the “Turnout so far adds” figure.
Voters changing sides: the move every county would need, all the same size, for the state to land on the poll. “Lopsided counties move less” spreads the same move so very one-sided counties change less. This is a requirement, not a prediction; the Rio Grande Valley and the suburbs have moved differently in past elections.
Counties with very irregular state counts (Culberson, Kenedy, Throckmorton, Winkler) should be read loosely. Early voting ends Friday; after that this reads as what Election Day has to look like.
