U.S. House elections, 2026

All U.S. House districts, including the 3rd Congressional District of Louisiana, are holding elections in 2026. The majority-vote system primary election is November 3, 2026. The general election is December 12, 2026. The filing deadline was August 7, 2026.[1]

The partisan primary and primary runoff, initially scheduled for May 16, 2026, and June 27, 2026, respectively, were canceled after the Supreme Court of the United States ruled in Louisiana v. Callais that the state's map for U.S. House seats was an unconstitutional gerrymander.[2][3] The initial filing deadline was February 13, 2026.

The outcome of this race will affect the partisan balance of the U.S. House of Representatives in the 120th Congress. All 435 U.S. House districts are up for election.

Currently, Republicans have a 219-214 majority with two vacancies in the U.S. House.[4][5] To read more about the U.S. House elections taking place this year, click here.

Candidates and election results


Beginning in the 2026 elections, Louisiana elections for U.S. Congress, the Louisiana Supreme Court, the Public Service Commission, and the Board of Elementary and Secondary Education use a closed partisan primary and primary runoff system. Candidates for those offices no longer run in majority-vote system primaries.


In 2026, the U.S. House races are taking place under the majority vote system after the partisan primaries scheduled for May 16 were canceled. All other offices that changed to the partisan primary system held partisan primaries.

General election

The primary will occur on November 3, 2026. The general election will occur on December 12, 2026. General election candidates will be added here following the primary.

Satellite spending

Satellite spending describes political spending not controlled by candidates or their campaigns; that is, any political expenditures made by groups or individuals that are not directly affiliated with a candidate. This includes spending by political party committees, super PACs, trade associations, and 501(c)(4) nonprofit groups.[6][7]

General election race ratings

See also: Race rating definitions and methods

Ballotpedia provides race ratings from four outlets: The Cook Political Report, Inside Elections, Sabato's Crystal Ball, and DDHQ/The Hill. Each race rating indicates if one party is perceived to have an advantage in the race and, if so, the degree of advantage:

  • Safe and Solid ratings indicate that one party has a clear edge and the race is not competitive.
  • Likely ratings indicate that one party has a clear edge, but an upset is possible.
  • Lean ratings indicate that one party has a small edge, but the race is competitive.[8]
  • Toss-up ratings indicate that neither party has an advantage.

Race ratings are informed by a number of factors, including polling, candidate quality, and election result history in the race's district or state.[9][10][11]

Prediction markets

See also: Prediction markets

A prediction market is a platform where users buy and sell contracts tied to the outcome of future events. The price of a contract reflects the market's estimated probability of that outcome — a contract priced at 55 cents suggests the market estimates a 55 percent chance the event occurs. Volume reflects how actively a market is being traded.[12] Unlike polls, which survey a sample of voters, prediction markets aggregate the financial stakes of individual bettors. A market with higher trading volume is generally considered more reliable than one with low volume, though prices can be influenced by large individual traders.

Post-filing deadline analysis

The following analysis covers all U.S. House districts up for election in Louisiana in 2026. Information below was calculated on Feb. 13, 2026, and may differ from information shown in the table above due to candidate replacements and withdrawals after that time.

In 2026, Louisiana and two other states — Alabama and Tennessee — reopened their filing deadlines for primary elections for the U.S. House of Representatives after the initial deadline passed. Each state reopened its filing deadline after passing new congressional maps ahead of the 2026 elections. Here's how this move affected primaries in Louisiana.

The filing deadline was initially Feb. 13, 2026. However, the filing deadline for all U.S. House districts was reopened until Aug. 7, 2026.

The 2026 election cycle was set to be the first in which Democratic and Republican candidates in the state ran in separate U.S. House primaries scheduled for May 16, 2026. Additionally, if no candidate received more than 50% of the votes cast, the top two candidates would have advanced to primary runoffs on June 27, 2026.

However, Louisiana instead used the system it used from 2014 through 2024, in which all candidates ran against each other on the same ballot. A candidate who received a majority of the votes cast for an office won outright. If no candidate reached that threshold, a second round of voting happened between the top two vote-getters.

On Jan. 31, 2024, a group of voters filed a lawsuit arguing that the 2024 congressional map containing a second majority-Black district was an unconstitutional racial gerrymander. On April 30, 2024, a federal district court agreed and enjoined the map's use in future elections. The state drew the 2024 map after a federal district court struck down a 2022 version containing one majority-Black district as a likely violation of Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act.

On April 29, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled Louisiana's 2024 map an unconstitutional racial gerrymander. As a result, Louisiana used the 2022 map in the 2026 elections.

Before Louisiana's filing deadline reopened, 32 candidates — 15 Democrats and 17 Republicans — filed to run for Louisiana's six congressional districts. That's 5.3 candidates per district.

After the deadline reopened, the number of candidates increased to 39 — tying with 2016 for the most candidates running for the U.S. House since 2014. Among those candidates were 14 Democrats, 20 Republicans, and five minor-party or independent candidates.

The number of candidates before the filing deadline reopened reflects only Democratic and Republican candidates since the state used partisan primaries at the time. The number of candidates after the filing deadline reopened accounts for candidates of all parties since all candidates ran on the same ballot.

There were four candidates per district in 2024, 3.7 in 2022, five in 2020, 4.7 in 2018, 6.5 in 2016, and 5.8 in 2014.

The number of open districts also increased from one to two. Rep. Julia Letlow (R-5th) announced before the deadline reopened that she would retire to run for the U.S. Senate. Rep. Cleo Fields (D-6th) announced he would retire to run for a seat in the Louisiana Legislature after the filing deadline reopened.

There was one open district in 2024, none in 2022, one in 2020, none in 2018, two in 2016, and one in 2014.

The number of incumbents who ran in contested primaries also increased from three — one Democrat and two Republicans — to four. Among those incumbents were one Democrat and three Republicans.

There were four incumbents in a contested primary in 2024, four in 2022, five in 2020, six in 2018, three in 2016, and three in 2014.

The total number of contested primaries decreased from nine — five Democratic and four Republican — to six. One factor that drove this change was that since all candidates ran on the same ballot, there were six possible primaries instead of 12.

In total, there were five contested primaries in 2024, four in 2022, six in 2020, six in 2018, five in 2016, and five in 2014.

Before the deadline reopened, the 5th Congressional District attracted the most candidates. Twelve candidates — five Democrats and seven Republicans — initially filed to run for the district. After the filing deadline reopened, nine candidates — three Democrats and six Republicans — filed to run.

Due to a decrease in candidacies in the 5th Congressional District and an increase in the 6th Congressional District, the latter ultimately attracted the most candidates.

Before the filing deadline reopened, five candidates — one Democrat and four Republicans — filed to run for the 6th Congressional District. After the deadline reopened, 10 candidates — three Democrats, six Republicans, and one Libertarian — filed to run.

Initially, the 2nd Congressional District was guaranteed to Democrats because no Republicans filed to run for the district. After the deadline reopened, one Republican filed to run in the district. As a result, no districts were guaranteed to either party since Democrats and Republicans filed to run in all six districts.

Partisan Voter Index

See also: The Cook Political Report's Partisan Voter Index

Heading into the 2026 elections, based on results from the 2024 and 2020 presidential elections, the Cook Partisan Voter Index for this district is R+18. This meant that in those two presidential elections, this district's results were 18 percentage points more Republican than the national average. This made Louisiana's 3rd the 39th most Republican district nationally.[14]
