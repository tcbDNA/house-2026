# 2026 governor races — sourced roster (retrieved 2026-10-28)

Built 2026-10-28 for the governor forecast. Every value in `roster_2026.csv` and `not_up_2026.csv`
comes from a file saved in this folder; `source_files` in each row names the files that support it.
Nothing was filled in from memory. Where a fact could not be sourced it is listed under
**Unverified** below.

### Column notes (`roster_2026.csv`)

- `incumbent_status`: `running` / `term-limited` / `retiring` / `lost primary`. Minnesota's Walz started a
  third-term campaign and ended it on Jan 5, 2026; that is recorded as `retiring`.
- `incumbent_elected_or_succeeded`: `elected (year)` if the incumbent first took office by winning a general
  election for governor; `succeeded (...)` if they took over mid-term (all six were lieutenant governors whose governor
  resigned), with any later wins.
- `d_nominee` / `r_nominee`: the name as printed on the official ballot or certified candidate list (see the
  `official_candidate_lists/` files). Lists that print "Last, First" were re-ordered to "First Last" without changing any
  token. All-caps lists (NM, OK, TX, VT) were changed to title case, and the curly quotes in Alaska's "JKT" became straight
  quotes. Arkansas prints titles as part of the ballot name ("Governor Sarah Huckabee Sanders", "State Senator Fred Love").
  Massachusetts ballots print only the team surnames ("HEALEY AND DRISCOLL"), so the full name comes from the official
  primary results and the printed form is shown in brackets.
  **Alaska** has no party nominees (top-four primary + ranked-choice general). `d_nominee` holds the only Democrat;
  `r_nominee` holds all three Republicans. The four general-election candidates are listed in `notes`.
  **California** (top-two) is one Democrat vs one Republican (Becerra vs Hilton).
- `other_candidates`: independents and third-party candidates with ≥10% in at least one public general-election
  poll whose fieldwork ended on or after 2026-09-01. The format is `name|party|max share|poll`, with entries separated by
  semicolons. The share is the poll's own published topline (see the discrepancies section for VoteHub differences).
  Candidates under 10% are mentioned in `notes` when relevant.
- `nominee_prior_offices`: governor races since 2010, plus every U.S. House and U.S. Senate run, for each D/R nominee
  and each ≥10% third-party candidate, as stated in the saved Wikipedia biographies and Ballotpedia profiles. "won" or
  "lost" refers to the general election unless a primary or convention is named. "None found" means neither saved
  source mentions such a run. Other offices are shown in parentheses for context only.

## Methods

1. **Race list and candidates.** Saved the Wikipedia overview "2026 United States gubernatorial elections" as rendered HTML
   and as raw wikitext (`action=raw`), and the Ballotpedia overview "Gubernatorial elections, 2026". Parsed the
   Wikipedia race-summary table and the Ballotpedia candidate table (name, party, status: On the Ballot / Withdrew / Lost
   Primary / Disqualified), then checked each D and R nominee against the infobox of that state's Wikipedia race page.
   All 36 race pages were saved as HTML and wikitext. Late changes were followed up with news articles: AK Begich withdrew
   and Taylor's certification was reversed; MI Duggan exited; RI McKee lost; MN Walz withdrew.
2. **Ballot names.** Two helper agents (same rules) saved the official certified candidate list or official sample ballot
   for each state into `official_candidate_lists/`. Where a state site blocked automated access (Cloudflare or anti-bot),
   they used a county or town sample ballot instead (see the table and Unverified). I spot-checked the AK, AR, KS, ME, MI, NH, NM, NV, NY, OK,
   RI, TX and WI files myself.
3. **Third-party ≥10% scan.** Saved VoteHub's governor dump (`votehub_governor_all_2026-10-28.json`, 673 polls) and listed
   every answer in a 2026 poll ending on or after 2026-09-01 that is not a D or R nominee and has ≥10%. I also parsed the
   general-election poll tables on all 36 Wikipedia race pages for rows dated September or October 2026, because
   VoteHub drops some third-party columns: it omits Colt Shelby in Arkansas. Each hit was then checked against the
   pollster's or sponsor's own release, which was saved under `polls/`. Polls from ActiVote, Rasmussen and Wedgewood (the
   project's excluded pollsters) played no role in any flag; none of them showed a third party at ≥10%.
4. **Incumbents.** Party and took-office date come from Wikipedia "List of current United States governors" (wikitext).
   Status comes from the overview's Retirements / Incumbents-defeated sections and the list's "(term limits)" and
   "(retiring)" flags, with news articles for IA, MN, WI and RI. Elected versus succeeded comes from the overview's
   "First elected" column and its footnotes (Ivey, Reynolds, Hochul, McKee, McMaster, Rhoden), checked against each
   governor's Wikipedia biography. The NGA roster (`nga_governors_2026-10-28.html`) is a second source for all 50 names.
5. **Prior offices.** Saved the Wikipedia biography (wikitext) of every nominee and incumbent with an article (91), and
   the Ballotpedia profile of every nominee who lacks one or has a sparse article (63). From each I extracted the infobox
   offices and the succession boxes ("X nominee for Governor/U.S. Senator ..."). I then read every sentence mentioning
   runs for Congress, the Senate or governor, and the Ballotpedia result rows for Governor, U.S. House and U.S. Senate.
6. **Not-up states.** Taken from the current-governors list, cross-checked with the 2023, 2024 and 2025 gubernatorial-election
   overview pages and the NGA roster.
7. Every download was saved exactly as served (no re-encoding), with `--compressed` decoding gzip only for Wayback copies.
   Parsing used `python3 -I` with scripts kept outside the repo (stdlib only; bs4, pdfplumber and pypdf are not installed).
   PDFs were read with the Read tool or with the helper agents' macOS PDFKit/Vision tools; Alabama's scanned certifications
   were read by OCR and confirmed against the SOS-hosted sample ballot.
8. **Final check.** `roster_2026.csv` was re-checked automatically against the saved sources (incumbent, party,
   took-office date, term-limited/retiring flags, and each nominee's surname in the state infobox and on the Ballotpedia
   on-ballot list). The 15 most competitive races (AZ GA MI WI PA NV KS IA OH ME NH NY MN AK NM) were also re-read by hand
   against the official lists and polls.

## Unverified / could not be sourced

- **No qualifying poll in CO, HI, WY.** Neither VoteHub nor the Wikipedia race pages list a general-election poll ending on
  or after 2026-09-01 for Colorado, Hawaii or Wyoming. The ≥10% third-party test cannot be applied there:
  `other_candidates` is empty and the `notes` say so. The candidate most likely to matter is Colorado's Greg Lopez
  (unaffiliated, former U.S. Rep.), and his standing is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Emerson RI with leaners.** VoteHub's figure of 38% for Block (48/38/12) cannot be checked against a saved file. The
  saved Emerson release gives 44/35/10 with 9% undecided, and says only that Foulkes "would reach about 48%" if undecideds
  break as they lean. The roster uses 35%.
- **UNH RI poll (Sep 17-21).** The PDF returned 403. The landing page is saved, but the 44/25/17 numbers come only from
  VoteHub and the Wikipedia table.
- **WaPo-Schar Maine topline PDF.** washingtonpost.com timed out or refused, and the Wayback copy returned 429. The saved
  crosstab workbook (a Google Sheets export linked from the WaPo page) carries the same governor question and is the
  source used.
- **"None found" prior offices.** These mean the saved Wikipedia article and/or Ballotpedia profile mentions no
  governor, U.S. House or U.S. Senate run. For nominees with no Wikipedia article (Bernadette Wilson, Gary Cordery,
  Terri Pickens, Zach Lahn, Aaron Guckian, Jerri Green, Amanda Janoo, Ken Casner, David Hatley, Colt Shelby), the only
  source is Ballotpedia, whose pre-2010 coverage is thin.
- **Massachusetts independent.** The official files print only "JAMES AND KITTREDGE". The first name "Andrea" comes from
  Ballotpedia and Wikipedia, not from an official file. Wikipedia says R Lt. Gov. nominee Shawn Oliver withdrew but stays
  on the ballot. The Milford sample ballot still prints "MINOGUE AND OLIVER", and no official withdrawal notice was found.
- **Rhode Island ballot strings.** No general-election candidate list or sample ballot could be fetched: vote.sos.ri.gov
  returned Cloudflare 403, archive copies are empty shells, and the Board of Elections has no general page. The D and R
  names come from the official Sep 9 primary results. Ken Block's entry is "Block, Kenneth" in the SOS declared-candidates
  report (Jul 30). The exact printed form is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Official lists from county/town ballots or non-state hosts.** The state election sites of AZ, GA, IA, MA, NV, NH and OH blocked automated
  access (Cloudflare, Incapsula, Akamai or another anti-bot check), and Idaho publishes no general-election list file. Their
  ballot names come from official county or town sample ballots or candidate lists, plus Idaho's state primary canvass.
  Wisconsin's Elections Commission PDF is an archive.org copy. Oregon's SOS directive is a copy hosted by Multnomah County. A county ballot shows the statewide
  governor contest identically, but it is not the state's certification.
